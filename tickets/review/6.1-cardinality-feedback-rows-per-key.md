# Cardinality feedback: consume rows per key and re-plan on a shift — implemented, for review

Second half of the rows-per-key slice. The observe ticket records, per table and equality column set, a running mean of the rows one key matched through a non-unique index. This ticket makes planning read that mean and re-plans cached statements when it moves far from what their plan assumed. Nothing module-facing changed.

## What was built

- **The seam** (`planner/stats/table-cardinality.ts`): `RowCountSources` now also extends a small `ObservedKeyCardinalities` interface (`observedRowsPerKey(table, columns)`), implemented by `CardinalityFeedbackManager` and forwarded by `databaseRowCountSources`; the manager's own inline sources object in `plannedBefore` answers `undefined`. One exported reader, `plannedRowsPerKey(table, columns, sources)`, applies the precedence: catalog statistics covering every column of the set (`rowCount > 0`, a `columnStats` entry per column by lowercase name, all-or-nothing like both modules' fallback) answer `undefined` so the module's statistics-backed estimate stands; otherwise `max(1, round(mean))` of the overlay's entry; otherwise `undefined`.
- **`IndexSeekNode`** snapshots that reader once in its constructor for a seek `equalitySeekKeyColumns` accepts, through `source.schemaManager?.getRowCountSources()` (so hand-built nodes in planner unit tests have no snapshot), exposes it as the readonly field `plannedRowsPerKey`, and relays it as `estimatedRows` in `computePhysical` in place of the module's number. The module's `filterInfo.indexInfoOutput.estimatedRows` is untouched, and the primary-key singleton branch is unaffected (a whole-key seek never has a snapshot). `getLogicalAttributes` adds `observedRowsPerKey` only when one answered, so plan snapshots over unobserved tables keep their shape.
- **The index-nested-loop candidate** (`rules/join/index-nested-loop.ts`) prices `rowsPerSeek` from `seek.plannedRowsPerKey ?? seekPlan.rows` of the seek it just rebuilt; the per-seek latency and both cost formulas read it. The module-currency admission gate in `probeModule` and `displacedPlan` keep reading the module's numbers.
- **Shift detection** in `recordRowsPerKey`: after a batch is folded, `rowCountSpread(mean, baseline) >= shiftFactor` resets the set's baseline to the mean, counts it in the set's `shifts`, logs under `core:cardinality-feedback` and calls `onShift(tableKey)`, so the existing statement marking and `FeedbackReplanState` cooldown apply unchanged.
- **The emit-time spec's baseline** (`rowsPerKeySpecOf` in `runtime/emit/scan.ts`) is now `plan.plannedRowsPerKey ?? module estimate`, so a plan priced from the mean does not shift on agreeing with it. The column-set key spelling moved into one helper, `columnSetKey` in `runtime/cardinality-observations.ts`, used by the spec and the manager's reader.
- **Docs**: `progressive-optimizer.md` (§3 opening re-measured, §3.1, §3.2, §3.3 rows-per-key precedence and the engine-side override, §3.4 shift rule, §3.6 third trigger, §3.7 ANALYZE / Parameters / Determinism, §5 trimmed, §6 key files); `optimizer-costing.md` (one paragraph after the module-selectivity rule, the seek-node paragraph, and the row-count section's "only the row count" sentence); `optimizer-joins.md` cost-model line for `rowsPerSeek`; `usage.md` option description; the backlog ticket's status paragraph.

## Re-measured motivating case

Memory backend, `parent` 2,000 rows, `child` 8,000 rows with four per parent under `child_parent (parent_id)`, never analyzed, `select p.name, c.note from parent p join child c on c.parent_id = p.id where p.name = 'p7'`, median of 11 runs, three repetitions:

| plan | median ms |
|---|---|
| hash join over a full scan of `child` (module prices the seek at 800 rows per key) | 9.9 to 11.2 |
| index-nested-loop seeking `child` (observed 4 rows per key) | 2.0 to 2.1 |

The plan-stage figures were 5.9 ms against 1.24 ms on a different run; §3 now carries 10.5 ms against 2.0 ms. The script lived in the session scratchpad and is not checked in.

## Tests

- `test/optimizer/cardinality-feedback.spec.ts`
  - record policy, "records rows per key only for a table planned above minRows, shifting on a 10x move of the mean from the planned baseline": pins the shift rule at the lowest layer (4 against a planned 2000 shifts, resets the baseline to 4 and counts one shift; 7 against 4 records silently) and the `observedRowsPerKey` reader for the recorded set and for an absent one.
  - precedence, "an observed rows per key reaches the seek and the join candidate unless the catalog covers the column set": with `child` recorded at 8,000 the join hashes; after a recorded mean of 4 for `parent_id`, `plannedRowsPerKey` answers 4 and the plan becomes `Join` + `IndexSeek`; after `analyze child` an entry recorded against the analyzed schema is held (`observedRowsPerKey` answers) but not consulted (`plannedRowsPerKey` is `undefined`); a by-value schema copy whose statistics lack one column of a composite set answers from the observation while the covered single column does not.

## Known gaps and things the reviewer should weigh

- **Eviction re-baseline is by inspection only.** A per-key set evicted by `maxKeySetsPerTable` and re-created while a cached plan was priced from the old mean takes that plan's snapshot as its baseline (the spec change above), so it does not shift spuriously; a set re-created while the plan was priced from the module's estimate may shift once. No test reaches the eviction-then-re-create path end to end.
- **Shift fan-out is per table, not per column set.** A statement that reads the table without seeking it re-plans once under the cooldown and lands the same plan; documented as an accepted cost in §3.6.
- **Catalog-wins precedence is a first rule.** A stale `ANALYZE` whose statistics cover the column set beats a fresher observed mean. Documented in §3.3 and on `plannedRowsPerKey` with the revisit condition (a measured case where the stale catalog loses a plan the mean would have won).
- **The override reaches every seek built with a schema manager**, including a plain `where col = ?` seek under a filter, not only the join candidate: the seek's advertised `estimatedRows` feeds sort costing, cache admission and aggregate sizing above it. That is what the ticket asked for ("the engine overrides where it consumes the estimate itself") but it widens the blast radius beyond joins; the full suite and the logic corpus (tables at most 200 rows, below `minRows`) cannot observe it.
- **Batched index-nested-loop and the mirrored candidate** are covered by inspection as the ticket lists them: the batched variant exists only over a seek proved to yield at most one row (never a snapshot), and the mirror goes through the same `tryIndexNestedLoop`.
- **Store-backed benchmarks were not gated.** The store suite's indexed benchmarks (`create index {table}_val`) run only under `QUEREUS_BENCH_LEVELDB=1`, which the default gate skips; a seek through such an index over a table above `minRows` could now re-plan after its first execution and change its counters. Not run here; re-baseline with a stated reason if the LevelDB profile ever shows a difference.

## Suggested review focus

- `plannedRowsPerKey` coverage rule against `equalityTupleSelectivity` (memory) and the store's arm estimate: the three must agree on when statistics "cover" a column set, or the override and the module's own estimate can disagree about the same seek.
- Whether relaying the override as `estimatedRows` for a non-join seek (point 4 above) needs its own test or a tripwire.
- Doc wording in §3.3 and the costing doc: both say modules are not fed observations and the module's `rows` rule is untouched; check that nothing in `module-authoring.md` (left at its word cap) now contradicts them.
